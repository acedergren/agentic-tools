# Natural Swedish Style

Use this as a working checklist, not as a grammar textbook. The goal is Swedish that a careful native reader would find clear, idiomatic, and suited to the situation.

## Revision Pass

1. Meaning and audience
   - Identify who is speaking, who is reading, and what should happen after reading.
   - Preserve facts, commitments, deadlines, and uncertainty markers.
   - Choose one register and keep it stable.

2. Sentence architecture
   - Main clauses normally use V2 word order: one sentence element before the finite verb.
   - Subordinate clauses place sentence adverbs such as `inte`, `alltid`, and `kanske` before the finite verb: `att vi inte har`, not `att vi har inte`.
   - Avoid long English-style noun chains. Prefer finite verbs, clauses, or Swedish compounds where they are natural.

3. Information flow
   - Put known context early and new or important information late.
   - Break overloaded sentences before stacking several `att`, `som`, or prepositional phrases.
   - Swedish often reads better with a concrete verb than with an abstract noun.

4. Definiteness, compounds, and forms
   - Check definite and indefinite forms: `en ny funktion`, `funktionen`, `den nya funktionen`.
   - Join true compounds: `kostnadsanalys`, `kunddata`, `språkstöd`. Split or rephrase only when the compound becomes heavy or ambiguous.
   - Prefer `de/dem` in formal writing. Use `dom` only when the requested voice is deliberately informal.

5. Verbs, particles, and prepositions
   - Check verb particles and prepositions by usage, not English analogy: `se till att`, `dra nytta av`, `bidra till`, `ha stöd för`.
   - Avoid overusing `kommer att`; Swedish often uses present tense, `ska`, `kommer att`, or a time expression depending on certainty and intent.
   - Prefer active constructions unless passive voice is natural for the genre or keeps attention on the process/result.

6. Vocabulary and idiom
   - Avoid direct Anglicisms when a plain Swedish verb works: `använda`, `stödja`, `förbättra`, `säkerställa`, `utreda`.
   - Do not over-polish everyday Swedish into stiff bureaucratic prose.
   - Use corpus or dictionary checks for collocations that feel plausible but uncertain.

7. Tone
   - Swedish professional tone is often direct, calm, and lightly understated.
   - `Vänligen` can sound stiff or irritated if overused; prefer a clear request when appropriate.
   - For product and support text, prefer `du` and concrete action verbs.
   - For public-sector or policy text, prefer klarspråk: clear structure, everyday words, explained terms, and reader-oriented headings.

8. Punctuation and typography
   - Swedish usually lowercases weekdays, months, languages, nationalities, and many title-like phrases unless they begin a sentence or are proper names.
   - Use commas for clarity, not as a mechanical copy of English punctuation.
   - Prefer Swedish quotation marks when producing polished final copy: `”...”`.
   - Check colon, hyphen, and abbreviation conventions when the text is formal or publishable.

9. Rhythm
   - Read the revised text silently as if spoken. Remove repeated scaffolding words and unnecessary filler.
   - Vary sentence length, but keep important instructions short.
   - Replace repeated starts such as `Det är viktigt att...` with a more direct subject and verb.

## Quality Gate

- Does it say the same thing as the source?
- Is the register coherent for the reader and medium?
- Are word order, definiteness, compounds, and prepositions idiomatic?
- Are any English source structures still visible?
- Would a native reader understand the point on the first pass?
- Did you avoid adding claims, promises, or intensity?
